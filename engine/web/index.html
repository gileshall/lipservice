How it sings

With a singer's own pitch, the voice's pitch moves on each note as one trained singer's did (VocalSet's singers; the seed picks which, and so that singer's own rate and depth of vibrato). Sung diction stresses every vowel. The changes are heard at the next Go.

Song format

A line each: bpm 90, rest 0.5 (beats), or a word and its notes, one a syllable, NOTE:BEATS (C4, F#3, Bb2), notes joined by + sharing a syllable. Lines starting # are comments.

A vibrato line between words writes the vibrato's size from that beat on, as a pedal is written beside a part's notes: 1 is the voice's own, 0 a straight tone. vibrato 0 jumps to none; vibrato 1.5:3 glides to one and a half times over the next 3 beats; several in a line follow one another, so vibrato 0 0:2 2:4 0:2 is none, held for 2 beats, grown to twice over 4, gone again over 2. The line takes no time in the song, and the last size holds until the next line. Of a singer's own pitch it sizes the vibrato and leaves the slower drift. In a choir each part has its own (Parts as song files); the piece Vibrato, Written shows it. The knob "vibrato alone" is what 1 comes to: every size written is multiplied by it.

Voices
How it sings

Each voice's pitch moves on its notes as one trained singer's did (VocalSet's singers, a different one for each voice, with that singer's own rate and depth of vibrato), each singer a touch off the beat and off the pitch, breaths taken in the rests with the note before shortened to make room. Each singer has a seat on a stage and the choir sounds in a room. Each voice sings its part as in the Sing tab: as written for a woman, an octave down for a man. The voices are spread over several workers and mixed as they are made.

Parts as song files

A crowd singing four chords round and round, each voice one note a chord. Drag on the pad: across sets one thing and up another. With the voices on an axis, the dashed line is how many this device sings at once, measured as it sings; past it the sound runs dry.

About

The engine is written in Rust and runs here as WebAssembly, in workers. Speech: the text is spelled out (numbers, dates) and each word read in its sense; a dictionary (CMU, BSD) and a model for the words not in it give the phones; a small model, taught by BERT, puts pauses where readers pause; a score model gives each sound its length, pitch and voice; a decoder turns that into a spectrum and Vocos (MIT) turns the spectrum into sound. Singing goes from the score through a source-filter vocoder. The voices are LibriTTS-R speakers (CC BY 4.0). The choir's pitch motion and breaths are learned from and cut from VocalSet (Wilkins, Seetharaman, Wahl and Pardo, ISMIR 2018; CC BY 4.0). SSML covers prosody (rate, pitch, volume), emphasis, breaks, say-as, phoneme and sub; a yes/no question rises at its end.

The sound keeps playing with the screen off where the browser allows it: the page asks for a playback audio session, and is fed by its workers' messages rather than timers.
